# Farsight Story and Example Map

This file maps potential examples into the book chapter by chapter.

The goal is to use a deliberate mixture of:
- **biblical stories**
- **human stories / testimony / pastoral examples**
- **short everyday examples**

The book will need all three.

If it uses only theology, it may feel abstract.
If it uses only human stories, it may feel sentimental or ungrounded.
If it uses only biblical stories, it may feel less embodied and contemporary.

The right mixture is:
- scripture to anchor truth
- human stories to embody it
- ordinary examples to make it recognizable

---

## Overall story strategy

### Use biblical stories for:
- theological weight
- interpretive correction
- showing that this theme is not invented, but biblical
- framing reality on God's terms

### Use human stories for:
- recognizability
- emotional access
- showing how this plays out in real discipleship
- making the reader feel implicated rather than merely instructed

### Use ordinary examples for:
- everyday credibility
- transitions
- showing that distorted sight happens in normal life, not only dramatic moments

---

## Ratio recommendation by book section

### Front third of book
Use more human and ordinary examples to help readers enter.

### Middle chapters
Use more biblical stories and scripture-heavy examples to anchor the theology.

### Final chapters
Return to a stronger mix of human and ordinary examples so the book lands in real life.

## Chapter 1 — On Fire
### Job of examples
Help readers recognize awakening, hunger, disruption, and the fading of spiritual intensity.

### Best biblical story options

#### 1. Emmaus road disciples (Luke 24)
**Why it fits:**
- hearts burning within them
- Jesus present before they fully recognized Him
- awakening language fits naturally
- great example of sudden spiritual clarity

**Best use:**
- chapter opening or section on moments of clarity

#### 2. Isaiah's vision and response (Isaiah 6)
**Why it fits:**
- vivid encounter with God
- conviction, exposure, and mission all follow
- shows awakening is not mere emotion; it reorders the person

**Best use:**
- section on awakening leading to surrender

#### 3. Paul's conversion (Acts 9)
**Why it fits:**
- dramatic awakening
- literal blindness/sight imagery
- powerful bridge between awakening and salvation

**Best use:**
- section on conversion as awakening

### Best human story options

#### 1. Byron's own healing-from-cancer story
**Why it fits:**
- strong personal authority if you want to use it
- naturally raises questions of fear, nearness of God, mortality, and changed priorities

**Best use:**
- if you want the opening to be personal and costly rather than generic

#### 2. A retreat / camp / conference experience story composite
**Why it fits:**
- highly relatable to many Christian readers
- easy way to establish shared experience

**Best use:**
- opening pages or section 1

#### 3. Shane Claiborne pre-conversion / awakening angle
**Why it fits:**
- if developed carefully, can show movement from vague spirituality to deeper reality

### Ordinary example options
- a sermon suddenly landing with unusual force
- prayer shifting from duty to contact
- a worship moment that briefly makes everything else feel small
- ordinary spiritual dullness returning after an intense season

### Recommendation
Use:
- one biblical awakening story
- one personal or composite human awakening story
- multiple everyday examples in the opening pages

## Chapter 2 — Nearsighted
### Job of examples
Expose what distorted spiritual sight looks like in practice.

### Best biblical story options

#### 1. Rich young ruler (Mark 10)
**Why it fits:**
- moral seriousness without surrendered sight
- earthly attachment reveals distorted vision
- perfect example of temporal value system colliding with eternal invitation

**Best use:**
- near the center of the chapter

#### 2. Martha distracted / Mary seeing what matters (Luke 10)
**Why it fits:**
- ordinary spiritual nearsightedness
- not obvious rebellion, but misordered urgency

**Best use:**
- section on misreading what matters most

#### 3. Jonah
**Why it fits:**
- God is real to him, but God's heart is not his heart
- exposes partial obedience + distorted perspective toward others

**Best use:**
- section on people as problems / public sinners / limited compassion

### Best human story options

#### 1. A believer panicking over money, status, or health while still using Christian language
**Why it fits:**
- no celebrity required
- common and exposing

**Best use:**
- early chapter illustration

#### 2. Mike L / moral failure
**Why it fits:**
- if handled carefully, can expose image management vs real transformation

#### 3. James D / loss of focus
**Why it fits:**
- useful if the story shows drift or outward success masking inward distortion

### Ordinary example options
- diagnosis changes tone of entire week
- inconvenience turns person into obstacle
- public sin in others feels outrageous while private pride feels normal
- abundance immediately interpreted as earthly improvement

### Recommendation
Use more ordinary examples here than long stories. This chapter works best when it feels uncomfortably familiar.

## Chapter 3 — Human Lenses
### Job of examples
Show why distorted sight feels natural from inside human life.

### Best biblical story options

#### 1. Peter rebuking Jesus about the cross (Matthew 16)
**Why it fits:**
- Peter sees through a human protective lens
- “This shall never happen to you” is exactly the kind of human realism this chapter describes

**Best use:**
- excellent anchor story for survival instinct and human perspective

#### 2. Job's friends
**Why it fits:**
- inherited theology, simplistic fairness, bad interpretation of suffering
- shows how painful certainty can masquerade as wisdom

**Best use:**
- pain/history/fairness section

#### 3. Samuel choosing by appearance before David is anointed (1 Samuel 16)
**Why it fits:**
- human instinct judges by externals
- God sees differently

**Best use:**
- transition toward God's lens

### Best human story options

#### 1. A trauma-shaped reaction story
**Why it fits:**
- shows that fear and pain become lenses
- should be careful, compassionate, and specific

#### 2. Parenting story
**Why it fits:**
- protector instinct, fear, and perceived fairness can all be explored naturally

#### 3. Everyday class/status/culture examples
**Why it fits:**
- reveals inherited assumptions and social discipleship

### Ordinary example options
- fear making control look wise
- upbringing making entitlement feel normal
- old betrayal making trust feel foolish
- comfort making obedience seem irresponsible

### Recommendation
Use one biblical story strongly here, probably Peter rebuking Jesus, because it captures human instinct colliding with divine purpose almost perfectly.

## Chapter 4 — God's Lenses
### Job of examples
Show what reality looks like when seen from God's perspective.

### Best biblical story options

#### 1. Joseph (Genesis 37–50)
**Why it fits:**
- human evil, long delay, suffering, purpose, and redemption all converging
- classic example of God seeing a larger story than humans do

**Best use:**
- suffering/purpose section

#### 2. Jesus with the rich, sick, grieving, sinful, and self-righteous
**Why it fits:**
- many mini-stories showing God in the flesh seeing differently than everyone else

**Best use:**
- section on how God sees people

#### 3. Jericho reframed carefully
**Why it fits:**
- powerful if handled with theological maturity
- can destabilize shallow readings of love and justice

**Warning:**
Do not use casually. High risk, high payoff.

### Best human story options

#### 1. Parent/child analogy
**Why it fits:**
- already natural to the manuscript
- good for explaining how limited perspective misreads wise severity

#### 2. Story of prolonged suffering later seen differently
**Why it fits:**
- helps keep theology from becoming abstract

### Ordinary example options
- skinned knee vs end of world analogy
- child demanding to inspect the parent's plan
- urgent crisis shrinking in meaning over time

### Recommendation
This chapter should lean more biblical than most. Joseph is probably the cleanest anchor story.

## Chapter 5 — Salvation
### Job of examples
Show salvation as decisive rescue that begins a larger work.

### Best biblical story options

#### 1. Exodus
**Why it fits:**
- salvation as rescue out of bondage into covenant life, not merely escape from danger
- rescue is clearly beginning, not ending

**Best use:**
- foundational framing for the whole chapter

#### 2. Prodigal son (Luke 15)
**Why it fits:**
- rescue into restored relationship
- not merely cancellation of penalty

**Best use:**
- relationship / being brought back to the Father

#### 3. Paul's conversion (Acts 9)
**Why it fits:**
- salvation as awakening
- blindness/sight motif already built in

**Best use:**
- section on salvation as awakening

### Best human story options

#### 1. Someone who remembers the moment of conversion clearly but then had to discover that the Christian life had only begun
**Why it fits:**
- very relatable to evangelical readers

#### 2. A testimony that contrasts “I got saved” language with later transformation
**Why it fits:**
- embodies the chapter's main correction

### Ordinary example options
- ticket vs journey
- doorway vs destination
- deposit vs full inheritance

### Recommendation
Use Exodus in some form. It naturally supports the chapter's core idea better than almost anything else.

## Chapter 6 — Sanctification
### Job of examples
Show how God changes people over time.

### Best biblical story options

#### 1. Peter after the resurrection
**Why it fits:**
- same man, different sight over time
- fear, failure, restoration, courage, and transformation

**Best use:**
- trust / maturity / fruit / renewal

#### 2. Abraham learning trust slowly
**Why it fits:**
- long process, not instant perfection
- good for growth through tension

#### 3. Romans 12 / renewing of mind (less story, more anchor text)
**Why it fits:**
- essential theological grounding

### Best human story options

#### 1. Story of slow growth rather than dramatic instant victory
**Why it fits:**
- supports the chapter’s realism

#### 2. Story where recovery time shortens over years
**Why it fits:**
- excellent embodiment of the manuscript's spiritual formation metric

#### 3. Story of suffering changing perspective over time
**Why it fits:**
- useful for sections on trust and pain

### Ordinary example options
- things that used to control you losing force
- quicker repentance
- slower panic
- more compassion and less image management

### Recommendation
Use a biblical character whose transformation took time, not one dramatic turnaround only. Peter is especially strong.

## Chapter 7 — Availability
### Job of examples
Show what life with renewed sight actually becomes.

### Best biblical story options

#### 1. Isaiah — “Here am I, send me” (Isaiah 6)
**Why it fits:**
- vision leads to availability
- one of the cleanest biblical examples for the chapter's core claim

#### 2. Matthew 25 (talents)
**Why it fits:**
- stewardship, entrusted life, responsibility, not hiding what is given

**Best use:**
- section on using time, gifts, and life for God's purposes

#### 3. 1 Corinthians 11
**Why it fits:**
- church selfishness vs Christ-centered participation
- excellent for section on availability through the church

### Best human story options

#### 1. A story of someone becoming interruptible for the sake of another person
**Why it fits:**
- availability becomes visible in ordinary sacrifice

#### 2. A ministry/service story that contrasts obligation vs love
**Why it fits:**
- aligns with existing comparison bank

#### 3. Someone serving faithfully without platform or recognition
**Why it fits:**
- supports fruit-over-image theme

### Ordinary example options
- calendar, budget, hospitality, inconvenience, service
- seeing a needy person as interruption vs assignment
- church involvement for recognition vs love

### Recommendation
Use Isaiah 6 and Matthew 25 prominently. They fit the chapter unusually well.

## Story placement guidance

### Avoid these mistakes

#### 1. Do not overload chapter openings with dramatic stories
One strong story is better than three partial ones.

#### 2. Do not use stories only as decoration
Each story should do argumentative work.

#### 3. Do not let human stories outrun theological clarity
Stories should embody the truth, not replace it.

#### 4. Do not rely only on famous stories
Some ordinary unnamed examples will be more effective.

## Bottom line

The book should use a mixture of biblical and human stories.

Not randomly.
Deliberately.

Biblical stories give the book authority.
Human stories give it blood.
Ordinary examples give it immediacy.

This mix will help the book stay both spiritually serious and recognizably human.
