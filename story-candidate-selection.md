# Farsight Story Candidate Selection

This file narrows human-story possibilities before drafting story paragraphs.

The goal is to decide, chapter by chapter, whether a slot should use:
- a **specific named person/story**
- an **anonymized real story**
- or a **shaped composite**

This keeps the manuscript from forcing specificity too early or using weak stories just to have a name.

---

## Selection principles

### Use a specific named person when:
- the story is strong
- the fit is obvious
- the person is appropriate to reference
- the story does argumentative work, not just emotional work

### Use an anonymized real story when:
- the story is strong but privacy or focus matters
- naming the person adds little
- the point is ordinary recognizability, not public association

### Use a shaped composite when:
- the experience is common and representative
- no single named person carries the chapter best
- the goal is broad reader recognition rather than biographical detail

## 2. Salvation
### Chapter role
Correct the nearsighted assumption that salvation is a one-time event and the goal, rather than the beginning.

### Best story type
**Specific real testimony if clearly available; otherwise shaped composite**

### Why
This chapter makes a sharper theological correction. It benefits from a testimony that can clearly embody:
- a real conversion moment
- later realization that salvation was the beginning, not the finish line

### Best current candidate from story list
**Jacob, atheist**

### Why Jacob may fit
- likely strong awakening/conversion arc
- potential for clear before/after contrast
- may naturally support salvation as entrance into reality

### Caution
This only works if the actual story includes:
- clear conversion or awakening
- later understanding of transformation / beginning
- not merely a change of opinion

### Strong fallback
**Shaped testimony composite** built from multiple evangelical-style conversion stories

### Recommended shape if composite
- “I got saved” language
- confidence anchored in the moment itself
- later discovery that God meant to transform the whole person, not simply resolve eternal risk

### Decision status
**Primary recommendation: Jacob if the story genuinely fits**
**Fallback: shaped composite testimony**

### Alternate option
A low-recognition faithfulness story involving long obedience without platform

**Fallback: unnamed real example held open until chosen**

## Questions to resolve later

Before final story drafting, these should be answered:

1. Which named stories are actually available and appropriate to use?
2. Which ones need anonymity?
3. Which story slots are better served by composite rather than biography?
4. Which stories carry emotional power without hijacking the chapter?

---

## Bottom line

The right move is not to force names into every chapter.

The right move is to choose the strongest form for each slot:
- composite where recognition matters most
- named testimony where the fit is genuinely strong
- anonymized real story where ordinariness and credibility matter more than profile

That will give the book more human weight without making it feel overproduced or manipulative.
