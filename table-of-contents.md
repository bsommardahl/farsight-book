# Farsight: Table of Contents and Chapter Synopsis

This file gives a one-page overview of the book's current structure.

## Working thesis

The Christian life is not mainly about behaving better in this world. It is about learning to see reality as God sees it and being prepared for eternity in His presence.

More fully:

Christians often interpret suffering, success, sin, purpose, death, and other people through a human, temporal, self-protective lens. But God is committed to renewing our vision. Salvation begins that restoration, sanctification deepens it, availability expresses surrendered participation in God's work, and farsight matures into increasing alignment with God's horizon.

---

## Book arc at a glance

The book moves through eight stages:

1. **Awakening** — moments when reality feels sharper than usual
2. **Diagnosis** — naming our spiritual nearsightedness
3. **Explanation** — why blurry vision feels normal to humans
4. **Corrective vision** — how God actually sees
5. **Rescue** — salvation as doorway, not finish line
6. **Renewal** — sanctification as changed sight over time
7. **Response** — availability as surrendered participation in God's work
8. **Visioned living** — farsight as learning to live from God's horizon

---

## Chapter 1 — On Fire
### Subtitle
**Awakening, hunger, and spiritual disruption**

### Chapter job
Awaken the reader to the possibility that spiritual highs were glimpses of clearer vision, not the goal itself.

### What it covers
- spiritual intensity
- hunger for God
- sudden clarity
- conversion and awakening experiences
- spiritual boredom after the fire fades
- the mistake of chasing the feeling instead of the reality it briefly revealed

### Governing question
When God feels vivid, is He merely giving us a spiritual experience — or is He showing us something we do not normally see?

### What it hands off to
If awakening gives us flashes of clearer sight, why is our ordinary sight so poor?

---

## Chapter 2 — Nearsighted
### Subtitle
**The problem of distorted sight**

### Chapter job
Show what spiritual nearsightedness looks like in real Christian life.

### What it covers
- temporal Christianity
- comfort and control as hidden idols
- treating people as obstacles
- misreading scripture through self-centered assumptions
- reducing sin to behavior management
- reducing salvation to a box to check
- nominal Christianity and practical atheism

### Governing question
What does it look like when believers speak eternal language but still interpret life through a temporary lens?

### What it hands off to
If our vision is distorted, why does that distortion feel so normal and persuasive?

---

## Chapter 3 — Human Lenses
### Subtitle
**Why blur feels normal**

### Chapter job
Explain why blurry spiritual vision feels normal to fallen human beings.

### What it covers
- creaturely limitation
- temporal experience
- survival and self-protection
- pain, history, trauma, and memory
- culture and upbringing
- desire and comfort
- pride and false narratives
- why human lenses make God seem confusing or severe

### Governing question
Why do fallen humans naturally interpret reality through fear, desire, and self-interest?

### What it hands off to
If our default lenses are this compromised, what does true sight actually look like?

---

## Chapter 4 — God’s Lenses
### Subtitle
**The true perspective**

### Chapter job
Show that God's perspective is the true lens and that His nature determines how He sees reality.

### What it covers
- God as eternal
- God as Creator
- God as love
- God as just
- God as never threatened, rushed, or confused
- God's perspective on suffering, people, purpose, and death
- God's eternal scale versus our immediate urgency

### Governing question
What does reality look like when it is seen from God's perspective rather than ours?

### What it hands off to
If God's perspective is true and we do not naturally see with it, how are we brought back into right relationship with Him at all?

---

## Chapter 5 — Salvation
### Subtitle
**The doorway, not the finish line**

### Chapter job
Show that salvation is essential rescue, but rescue into a larger process rather than the end of it.

### What it covers
- why Christians shrink salvation to a moment
- salvation as rescue into God's reality
- salvation as relationship, kingdom, and belonging
- salvation as awakening
- salvation as deposit, not full inheritance
- why treating salvation as the goal distorts everything after it
- being saved into sanctification, not merely from punishment

### Governing question
What if salvation is not the end of God's purpose for us, but the beginning of being brought back into what we were made for?

### What it hands off to
If salvation is the doorway, how does God begin changing those He has rescued?

---

## Chapter 6 — Sanctification
### Subtitle
**Vision renewal**

### Chapter job
Show how God actually changes a person from nearsightedness toward farsight.

### What it covers
- sanctification as more than self-improvement
- God's work rather than self-engineering
- perspective transplant
- changed trust, love, response to sin, suffering, and death
- fruit as evidence of renewed sight
- why sanctification is often painful
- less of me, more of Him
- preparation for eternity

### Governing question
How does God renew the sight of people He has rescued?

### What it hands off to
If God is renewing our sight, what does that renewed sight make possible in actual life?

---

## Chapter 7 — Availability
### Subtitle
**Surrendered participation**

### Chapter job
Show that renewed sight becomes availability to God, His people, and His purposes.

### What it covers
- clarity creating responsibility
- availability to the Holy Spirit
- deeper surrender over time
- using time, gifts, work, and suffering for God's purposes
- joining God in what He is already doing
- availability through the church
- availability toward the lost and overlooked
- celebrating real growth
- measuring life by fruit rather than image or intensity

### Governing question
What does a person do when their sight begins to change?

### What it leaves the reader with
What in my life is still unavailable to God?

---

## Chapter 8 — Farsight
### Subtitle
**Learning to see from God's horizon**

### Chapter job
Paint a picture of what it looks like to interpret life, people, suffering, time, and calling from something closer to God's perspective rather than the narrow urgency of the self.

### What it covers
- the problem of nearsighted living in the present moment
- God's whole perspective versus our fragmentary one
- why farsight is not spiritual arrogance or fake certainty
- how farsight changes our view of time, delay, suffering, obedience, people, and ordinary life
- how farsight restores proportion by placing us inside God's larger story
- how scripture, prayer, suffering, obedience, patience, and community form this kind of sight
- the cost and steadiness of seeing more truly
- what it means to live from God's horizon in practice

### Governing question
What becomes visible when I stop seeing only from my own small, immediate perspective?

### What it leaves the reader with
Am I willing to let God stretch my vision beyond myself?

## The book’s central distinctions

To keep the chapters from collapsing into each other, the current structure depends on these distinctions:

- **On Fire** = moments of awakening
- **Nearsighted** = what the problem looks like
- **Human Lenses** = why the problem feels normal
- **God’s Lenses** = what true sight is
- **Salvation** = what begins
- **Sanctification** = what continues
- **Availability** = surrendered participation in God's work
- **Farsight** = the grace of learning to live from God's horizon

---

## Strong recurring motifs

These motifs should appear throughout the book, but in controlled rather than repetitive ways:

- this life is short; eternity is real
- we mistake blur for normal sight
- human beings default to temporal, self-protective seeing
- God sees truly because of who He is
- salvation begins rescue
- sanctification renews sight
- availability is surrendered participation in God's work
- farsight is the grace of learning to live from God's horizon
- fruit reveals real transformation

---

## Supporting device: “Which is better? A or B”

A recurring framework in the book compares lenses the way an eye doctor compares options:

- Which is better?
- A or B?
- Which lens is clearer?

This device helps readers compare:
- the familiar lens of nearsightedness
- the truer lens of God's perspective

It works best when used selectively and when both options initially feel plausible.
