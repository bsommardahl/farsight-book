# Which Is Better? A or B

This file develops a recurring book device based on the eye doctor experience:

- "Which is better?"
- "A or B?"
- "This one, or this one?"

The point is not merely comparison for its own sake.

The point is that people often live with blurry vision for so long that blur feels normal. The eye doctor does not invent vision. He helps expose which lens is clearer.

That fits the book's core argument perfectly.

---

## Why this device works

### It is familiar
Almost everyone has experienced the eye exam rhythm, or at least understands it intuitively.

### It is concrete
It takes the sight metaphor out of abstraction.

### It invites participation
Instead of just telling readers they are wrong, it lets them compare two ways of seeing.

### It fits the whole book
The book is already about vision, clarity, distortion, lenses, nearsightedness, and renewed sight.

---

## The core idea behind the device

The recurring question is not simply:

> Which option do you prefer?

It is deeper:

> Which lens is clearer?
> Which way of seeing is truer?
> Which option feels natural because of human nearsightedness, and which one reflects God's perspective?

This matters because many Christian confusions are not open rebellions. They are blurry preferences treated as if they were clear sight.

---

## Best use of the device

Recommended use:

### 1. As recurring mini-sections inside chapters
Not as a gimmick on every page, but as a strategic pattern that appears where contrast helps expose false assumptions.

### 2. As a transition tool
Use it when moving from familiar human instinct to clearer divine perspective.

### 3. As a diagnostic tool
Use it to show how both options may sound plausible at first, but one is still blurrier.

---

## Warning

Do not make this shallow.

If every contrast feels like:
- bad option / good option
- obviously wrong / obviously right
- cartoonish flesh / cartoonish holiness

then the device becomes childish and predictable.

The best contrasts are the ones where:
- both options initially sound reasonable
- one is more natural to fallen instinct
- one is clearer, truer, and more eternal
- the clearer one is often also more costly

## Contrast bank: raw A/B prompts

These are not final. They are idea seeds.

## Core vision contrasts

- Which is better? A lens that feels familiar, or a lens that is actually clear?
- Which is better? Immediate comfort, or truthful sight?
- Which is better? Seeing what is near, or seeing what is real?
- Which is better? A life organized around urgency, or a life ordered by eternity?

## Nearsightedness / diagnosis

- Which is better? Life going well now, or being prepared for eternity?
- Which is better? Treating people as obstacles, or seeing them as souls?
- Which is better? Behavior management, or heart transformation?
- Which is better? Avoiding visible sins, or letting God expose the roots beneath them?
- Which is better? A faith that improves my image, or a faith that destroys my illusions?
- Which is better? Public righteousness, or real surrender?

## Human lenses

- Which is better? Self-protection, or trust?
- Which is better? Instinct, or truth?
- Which is better? A lens formed by fear, or a lens formed by God?
- Which is better? The assumptions I inherited, or the ones God is teaching me?
- Which is better? Feeling safe, or seeing clearly?
- Which is better? A comforting lie, or a healing truth?

## God's lenses

- Which is better? A god small enough to fit my preferences, or God as He truly is?
- Which is better? A definition of love based on comfort, or one based on holiness and truth?
- Which is better? My sense of fairness, or God's justice?
- Which is better? Panic in the present, or trust in the eternal God?
- Which is better? A life interpreted by pain alone, or a life interpreted by God's purposes?

## Salvation

- Which is better? A box checked for later, or rescue that begins now?
- Which is better? Escaping hell, or entering God's reality?
- Which is better? Religious reassurance, or actual reconciliation with God?
- Which is better? Getting forgiven and staying the same, or being brought into new life?

## Sanctification

- Which is better? Trying harder, or being changed?
- Which is better? Looking mature, or becoming mature?
- Which is better? Producing religious behavior, or bearing spiritual fruit?
- Which is better? Managing sin, or learning to hate it?
- Which is better? Demanding explanations, or learning trust?
- Which is better? Protecting the old self, or letting God replace your lens?

## Availability

- Which is better? Staying comfortable, or being useful to God?
- Which is better? Guarding my life, or offering it?
- Which is better? Serving for recognition, or serving because love sees need?
- Which is better? Asking how little I can give, or asking what love requires?
- Which is better? Being admired as spiritual, or being available to the Spirit?

## Strong comparisons already present in the repo

These come largely from `graveyard/stories.md` and should be mined aggressively:

- Living well vs preparing for eternity
- Being spiritual vs discovering and embracing spiritual reality
- Persecution is avoidable vs persecution is desired because it transforms
- Making God King vs realizing God was always King and we were blind
- Behaving well vs surrendering to God
- Studying for knowledge or superiority vs studying for God's approval
- Wanting heaven for the mansion vs wanting heaven for God's presence
- Loving others because it's expected vs loving them because we see them as God sees them
- Serving in church because it is expected vs serving because it participates in God's work
- Pray for healing vs pray for peace
- Following Jesus for fanfare or earthly benefit vs following Him because He leads to eternity
- Want the Holy Spirit for gifts vs want Him for transformation
- Giving for recognition vs giving because there is need
- Abstaining from sin to avoid punishment vs abstaining because sin wastes your life
- Repenting to avoid losing heaven vs repenting because you crave transformation
- Enraged by public sin vs moved by compassion for the lost
- Hearing a sermon because you have to vs hearing it because God may renew your mind through it
- Fearing judgment because secrets will be exposed vs receiving judgment because it leads to purity
- Acting righteous for human approval vs being made righteous by God

## Bottom line

This is a strong device because it does what the book needs:

- it exposes blur
- it honors the sight metaphor
- it helps readers compare lenses
- it turns abstract theology into a felt act of discernment

Used well, it will make the book more memorable and more examinable.
