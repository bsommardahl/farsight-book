## Availability is the response to renewed sight

What should happen when a person starts to see more clearly?

Not just in theory. Not just in prayer journals. Not just in moments of conviction.

If God really is renewing our sight, then the obvious next question is not, "Wasn't that powerful?"

It is, "What am I now available for that I was resisting before?"

That question matters because spiritual clarity can be admired without being obeyed. A person can have insight, conviction, language, even tears, and still remain largely unavailable to God. They can agree with truth while continuing to guard their schedule, their comfort, their money, their image, their plans, and their relationships from the reach of surrender.

But clearer sight is meant to change availability. If God is showing us reality more truthfully, then He is not merely informing us. He is calling us.

Availability is the proper response to renewed sight.

If God is renewing our vision, then the faithful answer is not admiration from a distance. It is surrendered participation. Availability means letting God keep working on us, refusing to return to self-protective living, joining God in what He is already doing, and learning to measure life by fruit rather than image, intensity, or activity alone.

## Section 1: Clarity creates responsibility

### Main point
Seeing more clearly is not meant to terminate in self-congratulation.

### Ideas to include
- clearer sight increases accountability
- insight without response hardens into self-deception
- the point is not to admire truth but align with it
- seeing creates obligation to yield

### Useful source ideas
- God gives us time, but expects us to grow up
- discernment and judgment themes from `graveyard/lessons.md`
- not an opportunity to relax; stay available to God’s work

### Expanded draft
When God gives a person clearer sight, He is not offering a collectible spiritual experience. He is exposing them to reality so they can respond to it.

That means clarity creates responsibility. The more clearly we see, the less excuse we have for continued halfheartedness. Insight without response does not remain innocent for long. It hardens into self-deception.

This is one reason discernment matters so much in the Christian life. To see what God is revealing and then continue as though nothing has changed is not maturity. It is delay dressed up as caution. God gives people time, yes, but He gives that time for growth, not for indefinite avoidance.

### Candidate A/B contrast
- Which is better? Admiring the truth, or yielding to it?

## Section 2: Become available to the Holy Spirit

### Main point
Availability begins with surrender to God's ongoing work within us.

### Ideas to include
- stop resisting exposure
- stop managing image
- stop asking God to bless self-protective arrangements
- invite the Spirit to continue revealing, correcting, pruning, and forming

### Useful source ideas
- become available to the Holy Spirit
- let the HS make you even more available
- sanctification is God's work, not ours

### Human story insertion draft
A woman leaves church already thinking about the rest of her day. There are errands to run, messages to answer, dinner to figure out, maybe a little margin if everything goes well. Then she notices someone lingering at the edge of the room — not dramatic, not urgent in the obvious sense, just quietly alone in a way that would be easy to ignore. She stops, stays longer than she planned, listens, prays, and lets the rest of the day bend around that moment.

Nothing outwardly heroic happened. No platform, no spotlight, no story impressive enough to retell for years. But that small interruption revealed something important: her life was becoming less protected from love. That is availability.

### Expanded draft
The first answer to renewed sight is not activism. It is surrender.

A person who begins to see clearly must decide whether they actually want God to continue. Many want enough light to feel stirred, but not enough light to expose the habits, motives, attachments, and comforts they still worship.

To become available to the Holy Spirit is to stop asking for inspiration while resisting intrusion. It is to stop asking God to encourage us while refusing to let Him contradict us. It is to stop treating His work like a guest appearance in a life we still intend to control.

### Candidate A/B contrast
- Which is better? Being admired as spiritual, or being available to the Spirit?

## Section 3: Let God make you more available

### Main point
Availability is not static. The Spirit keeps enlarging it.

### Ideas to include
- God often asks for deeper surrender over time
- availability grows as trust grows
- He may touch schedule, money, ambitions, relationships, service, reputation, comfort
- as sight sharpens, resistance becomes more obvious

### Useful source ideas
- less of me, more of Him
- God’s perspective replaces ours over time
- move out of the way

### Expanded draft
Availability is not a one-time yes. It is a repeated surrender.

God may first confront obvious sins, but He rarely stops there. He keeps pressing into whatever remains guarded, defended, or self-owned. He keeps asking whether we want transformation or only relief. He keeps exposing the parts of life we still call ours in a way that quietly means unavailable.

That is why availability grows in layers. At first, a person may surrender obvious rebellion. Later, God touches comfort. Then image. Then ambition. Then money. Then schedule. Then relationships. Then the need to stay impressive, protected, or in control.

As sight sharpens, resistance becomes easier to see. What once felt like personality starts looking like self-protection. What once felt like wisdom starts looking like fear. What once felt like harmless preference starts looking like a guarded life.

That is not condemnation. It is mercy. God is not trying to make people less alive. He is trying to make them less defended against love, truth, obedience, and usefulness.

### Candidate A/B contrast
- Which is better? Guarding my life, or offering it?
- Which is better? Asking God to leave my protected areas alone, or letting Him keep making me available?

## Section 4: Availability changes how we use our lives

### Main point
When we stop treating our lives as private property, everything becomes available for God's purposes.

### Ideas to include
- time
- money
- attention
- gifts
- suffering
- work
- relationships
- ordinary opportunities

### Useful source ideas
- my service / life / time on earth
- talents / stewardship theme from Matthew 25
- God has us here for a purpose

### Biblical anchor note: Matthew 25
Matthew 25 fits this section because entrusted things are not meant to be hidden, preserved, or treated as private possessions. They are given for faithful use. That connects naturally to the chapter’s claim that renewed sight changes how we think about time, gifts, resources, and responsibility.

### Expanded draft
A nearsighted person asks, “How much of this do I have to give?” A person being renewed asks, “What is this for?”

That is a massive shift.

Time, money, gifts, work, energy, and even pain begin to look different when they are no longer organized around self-preservation alone. We stop treating our lives like private property and begin receiving them as entrusted things.

Matthew 25 sharpens this. What is entrusted by the master is not given merely to be protected from risk. It is given to be used faithfully. In the same way, a life being renewed by God no longer asks only how to stay safe. It begins asking how to be faithful with what has been given.

That is why availability always becomes concrete. It touches calendars, budgets, habits, conversations, attention, hospitality, service, inconvenience, and risk. The person who sees more clearly starts to realize that nothing in life is merely "mine" in the old sense anymore.

### Candidate A/B contrast
- Which is better? Asking how little I can give, or asking what love requires?

## Section 5: Join God in what He is already doing

### Main point
Availability is participation, not self-invented heroism.

### Ideas to include
- God is already at work
- we are invited into His purposes, not the other way around
- the question is not how to build our own spiritual project
- the question is how to join what He is doing

### Useful source ideas
- join God in what He is doing
- the kingdom already exists; we wake up to it
- helping others see and join it

### Expanded draft
One of the most freeing things about spiritual maturity is realizing that God does not need our inventions nearly as much as He wants our availability.

He is already working in people, in churches, in suffering, in scripture, in conviction, in the quiet rearranging of lives. We are not asked to become impressive. We are asked to join Him.

That is an important correction because many Christians still imagine the mature life as building something noticeable for God. They want a role, a platform, a ministry identity, a visible assignment that proves usefulness. But availability is not self-invented heroism. It is surrendered participation.

God's kingdom already exists. His purposes are already moving. His Spirit is already at work. The mature question is not, “How do I create something spiritual enough to matter?” The mature question is, “Where is God already moving, and am I willing to join Him there?”

That kind of participation is usually less glamorous than people expect. It may look like faithfulness, presence, listening, service, generosity, risk, endurance, hidden obedience, or helping one person see more clearly than they did before. But that is enough. God is not asking us to be central. He is asking us to be available.

### Candidate A/B contrast
- Which is better? Building my own spiritual project, or joining God’s work already in motion?
- Which is better? Trying to become impressive for God, or becoming available to Him?

## Section 6: Availability through the church

### Main point
Availability is not merely private. It includes how we belong to and serve Christ's body.

### Ideas to include
- church is not a religious vendor
- church service is not performance or duty alone
- selfishness damages the body
- maturity includes seeing the needs of others
- gathered worship and shared life must not become self-centered

### Useful source ideas
- 1 Corinthians 11 notes from `graveyard/lessons.md`
- despising the church by humiliating the poor
- God wants the spotlight
- putting others first
- this is not just about the Lord's Supper, but about shared life

### Expanded draft
A person who is becoming available to God cannot remain casually self-centered in the church.

To belong to Christ's body while using it for recognition, comfort, networking, or selective participation is to keep pulling spiritual reality back into the orbit of self. That is one reason selfishness in the church is so spiritually serious. It does not merely create inconvenience. It damages the body, humiliates people, and moves the focus away from Jesus.

Availability means learning to show up with Jesus, not self, at the center. It means asking not simply what I prefer, but what love requires. It means recognizing that gathered life with other believers is not a stage for spiritual image management, but a place where God keeps teaching us to put others first.

### Candidate A/B contrast
- Which is better? Serving because it is expected, or serving because love sees need?

## Section 7: Availability toward the lost and the overlooked

### Main point
Clearer sight changes how we see people who are far from God.

### Ideas to include
- the lost are not interruptions, threats, or annoyances
- nearsightedness reacts with judgment, disgust, or avoidance
- farsight sees need, blindness, and opportunity for love
- being available means being interruptible

### Useful source ideas
- finding the lost, teaching them about Him
- seeing the lost with compassion
- unlovable people as treasures
- enraged by public sin vs compassionate toward the lost

### Expanded draft
A person whose sight is being renewed can no longer treat lost people merely as evidence that the world is getting worse.

That is one of the ugliest habits of shallow Christianity. It learns to talk about the lost with irritation, disgust, or superiority instead of grief, courage, and love. It treats public sin mainly as something to be offended by. It uses other people's obvious lostness to reinforce its own sense of distance from them.

But clearer sight changes that.

The lost begin to look less like enemies to mock or avoid and more like people trapped in blindness. The overlooked begin to look less like background scenery and more like treasures God has not stopped caring about. The difficult begin to look less like interruptions and more like part of the field in which love must become concrete.

Availability means we stop asking only whether people are convenient, agreeable, respectable, or likely to improve our lives. We begin asking whether love is being withheld because our sight is still too small.

This does not mean sentimentality. It does not mean pretending evil is not evil. It means that obvious lostness becomes a summons to compassion rather than an excuse for distance.

### Candidate A/B contrast
- Which is better? Being enraged by the lost, or being available to love them?
- Which is better? Using people’s obvious brokenness to confirm your distance, or seeing it as a summons to compassion?

## Section 8: Celebrate every new view

### Main point
Growth should be noticed and received with gratitude.

### Ideas to include
- celebrate new clarity without idolizing self
- thank God for each place where old blur loses power
- growth can be gradual and still real
- changed reflexes are worth naming

### Useful source ideas
- celebrate every new view
- spiritual formation measured in recovery time
- evidence of renewed sight

### Stub
Not every change arrives like a revival. Sometimes the sign of growth is that fear loosens faster, repentance comes quicker, compassion rises sooner, or worldly measures lose some of their glamour. Those are not small things. They are new views, and they should be received with gratitude.

### Candidate A/B contrast
- Which is better? Chasing dramatic experiences, or noticing real transformation?

## Section 9: Measure by fruit, not image

### Main point
The final measure of availability is not intensity, platform, or visible busyness, but fruit.

### Ideas to include
- fruit of the Spirit as evidence
- not gifts, not hype, not reputation alone
- not how “on fire” we look
- not how many roles we fill
- the deeper question is whether love, joy, peace, patience, and the rest are becoming visible

### Useful source ideas
- measure by the fruit of the Spirit
- seek gifts more than fruits
- fruit as evidence of what God is doing

### Expanded draft
At the end of all the language about surrender, service, mission, and clarity, the real question remains simple: what kind of person is being formed?

The most persuasive evidence that someone is available to God is not spiritual noise. It is fruit. Not platform. Not intensity. Not busyness. Not the ability to sound deep. Not the impression of being "on fire."

Fruit tells the truth. Love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control are not decorations hung on a stubborn self. They are evidence that the Spirit has gained real room in a life.

### Candidate A/B contrast
- Which is better? Looking active, or bearing fruit?

## Final call: clearer sight should end in yieldedness

Isaiah 6 captures the logic of this chapter with unusual clarity. Isaiah sees, he is undone, he is cleansed, and then he is sent. Vision leads to exposure. Exposure leads to cleansing. Cleansing leads to availability. The point is not activism for its own sake. It is that a person who truly sees God cannot remain untouched or unavailable.

That is the burden of this chapter.

To see more clearly is to become less guarded, less self-owned, less impressed by our own plans, and more interruptible by God. Availability is not passivity. It is active surrender. It is the refusal to keep treating our lives as closed systems arranged around self-protection.

And it is not the end of the movement.

Availability is what prepares us for the next grace: not only to be usable by God, but to increasingly perceive life from His horizon rather than our own. Surrender makes us teachable. It loosens our grip on the self. It creates room for a wider vision.

So the question this chapter leaves behind is simple and severe:

What in my life is still unavailable to God?

That question is not a threat. It is an invitation. It is the doorway into the next movement of maturity: learning not only to yield to God's work, but to see more and more of reality in the light of His purposes.
