# Farsight Book Architecture Pass

This file evaluates the current chapter set as one book rather than as separate documents.

The goals of this pass are:
- tighten chapter roles
- identify overlap and repetition
- confirm or improve chapter order
- clarify transitions
- make sure the book reads like one argument

---

## Current structure snapshot

### Chapter 1 — `on-fire.md`
**Role:** experiential doorway

### Chapter 2 — `near-sighted.md`
**Role:** diagnosis of the problem

### Chapter 3 — `human-lenses.md`
**Role:** explanation of why the problem feels normal

### Chapter 4 — `god-lenses.md`
**Role:** presentation of the true lens / corrective vision

### Chapter 5 — `salvation.md`
**Role:** rescue into God's reality; the beginning, not the finish line

### Chapter 6 — `sanctification.md`
**Role:** how God renews our sight over time

### Chapter 7 — `availability.md`
**Role:** lived response; what renewed sight looks like in practice

This is a strong order.

It moves from:
- felt experience
- to diagnosis
- to explanation
- to corrective vision
- to redemptive entry
- to transformation
- to lived response

That is a coherent and compelling book arc.

---

## Recommended chapter order

Keep the current order.

### Why this order works

## 1. `On Fire`
Starts where many believers actually live: hunger, awakening, spiritual intensity, and confusion.

## 2. `Nearsighted`
Names the problem directly once readers are emotionally inside the conversation.

## 3. `Human Lenses`
Explains the underlying mechanics of the problem.

## 4. `God's Lenses`
Prevents the book from becoming merely therapeutic or introspective by turning the reader toward God Himself.

## 5. `Salvation`
Shows how rescue begins, once the reader has seen both the problem and the true vision.

## 6. `Sanctification`
Answers what happens after rescue: renewed sight over time.

## 7. `Availability`
Closes with embodiment, obedience, mission, and fruit.

No reorder is needed right now.

## Tightened chapter roles

One of the main risks in a project like this is that several chapters start saying the same thing in slightly different words.

To avoid that, each chapter needs a sharply distinct job.

## Chapter 1 — On Fire
**Do:**
- open with lived experience
- honor spiritual awakening
- question emotional nostalgia
- reframe “fire” as possible moments of clearer sight

**Do not drift into:**
- full diagnosis of nearsightedness
- heavy theology of sanctification
- too much critique too early

**One-sentence job:**
Awaken the reader to the possibility that their spiritual highs were glimpses of clearer vision, not the goal itself.

---

## Chapter 2 — Nearsighted
**Do:**
- diagnose distorted Christian seeing
- name symptoms clearly and sharply
- expose temporal assumptions
- confront nominality carefully

**Do not drift into:**
- long explanations of why human beings are like this
- extended theology of God's attributes
- too much solution language

**One-sentence job:**
Show the reader what spiritual nearsightedness looks like in real Christian life.

---

## Chapter 3 — Human Lenses
**Do:**
- explain the formation of distorted sight
- make the problem understandable from inside human experience
- describe fear, desire, history, culture, pride, and limitation as lens-formers

**Do not drift into:**
- re-diagnosing nearsightedness at length
- full presentation of God's perspective
- too much moral condemnation

**One-sentence job:**
Explain why blurry spiritual vision feels normal to fallen human beings.

---

## Chapter 4 — God's Lenses
**Do:**
- establish that God's perspective is true because of who God is
- ground the book in theology proper
- show love, justice, eternity, purpose, and clarity in God

**Do not drift into:**
- too much sanctification language
- overexplaining human psychology again
- turning into an abstract doctrinal lecture disconnected from life

**One-sentence job:**
Show that God's perspective is the true lens and that His nature determines how He sees reality.

---

## Chapter 5 — Salvation
**Do:**
- correct the reduction of salvation to a moment or finish line
- frame salvation as rescue into God's reality
- connect salvation to relationship, kingdom, and beginning

**Do not drift into:**
- becoming a duplicate of sanctification
- sounding like salvation is downgraded
- getting lost in technical theological debates unless necessary

**One-sentence job:**
Show that salvation is essential rescue, but rescue into a larger process rather than the end of it.

---

## Chapter 6 — Sanctification
**Do:**
- explain how God renews sight over time
- make transformation practical and theological
- show fruit, pain, trust, love, and reordering

**Do not drift into:**
- repeating the entire God-lenses chapter
- becoming a generic holiness chapter
- overlapping too much with the practical service emphasis of Availability

**One-sentence job:**
Show how God actually changes a person from nearsightedness toward farsight.

---

## Chapter 7 — Availability
**Do:**
- land the book practically
- define response, participation, church life, mission, and fruit
- show what renewed sight does in real life

**Do not drift into:**
- re-explaining sanctification at length
- becoming mere activism
- sounding like a ministry recruitment speech

**One-sentence job:**
Show that renewed sight becomes availability to God, His people, and His purposes.

## Major overlap to watch

These are the main places where future drafting could become repetitive.

## Overlap 1: `near-sighted.md` and `human-lenses.md`

### Risk
Both chapters can talk about distorted perception, fear, comfort, self-protection, and temporal thinking.

### Fix
- `near-sighted` should stay **symptomatic and diagnostic**
- `human-lenses` should stay **explanatory and formative**

### Simple distinction
- `near-sighted` = what it looks like
- `human-lenses` = why it feels normal

---

## Overlap 2: `god-lenses.md` and `sanctification.md`

### Risk
Both can talk about God's perspective, learning trust, suffering, and seeing differently.

### Fix
- `god-lenses` should focus on **what God's perspective is**
- `sanctification` should focus on **how God grows that perspective in us**

### Simple distinction
- `god-lenses` = the content of true sight
- `sanctification` = the process of receiving true sight

---

## Overlap 3: `salvation.md` and `sanctification.md`

### Risk
Both chapters can talk about beginnings, transformation, new life, and restored sight.

### Fix
- `salvation` should emphasize **entry, rescue, deposit, doorway**
- `sanctification` should emphasize **ongoing renewal, fruit, pain, growth**

### Simple distinction
- `salvation` = what begins
- `sanctification` = what continues

---

## Overlap 4: `sanctification.md` and `availability.md`

### Risk
Both can talk about surrender, obedience, fruit, and life change.

### Fix
- `sanctification` should emphasize **God’s inward transforming work**
- `availability` should emphasize **our lived outward response and participation**

### Simple distinction
- `sanctification` = how God changes us
- `availability` = how changed people live

---

## Repeated motifs that should stay, but be controlled

These motifs are good. They belong in the book. But they need disciplined repetition rather than re-explanation in every chapter.

### 1. This life is short; eternity is real
Use as a governing drumbeat, not a fresh thesis every chapter.

### 2. Human beings interpret life through temporal lenses
Introduce strongly, then callback briefly.

### 3. God is preparing us for eternity
Explain once deeply, then reinforce.

### 4. Sanctification is renewed sight
Treat this as a central definition, not a sentence every few paragraphs.

### 5. Sin is waste, not merely rule-breaking
Strong idea — repeat selectively.

### 6. Fruit reveals real transformation
Use as evidence language, especially in `sanctification` and `availability`.

## Chapter transition recommendations

To make the book feel seamless, each chapter should end by naturally handing the reader to the next.

## 1 → 2
### From `On Fire` to `Nearsighted`
**Transition idea:**
If awakening gives us flashes of clearer sight, then why is our ordinary sight so poor?

## 2 → 3
### From `Nearsighted` to `Human Lenses`
**Transition idea:**
If our vision is distorted, why does that distortion feel so normal and persuasive?

## 3 → 4
### From `Human Lenses` to `God's Lenses`
**Transition idea:**
If our default lenses are unreliable, then what does true sight actually look like?

## 4 → 5
### From `God's Lenses` to `Salvation`
**Transition idea:**
If God's perspective is true and we do not naturally see with it, how are we brought back into right relationship with Him at all?

## 5 → 6
### From `Salvation` to `Sanctification`
**Transition idea:**
If salvation is the doorway, how does God begin changing those He has rescued?

## 6 → 7
### From `Sanctification` to `Availability`
**Transition idea:**
If God is renewing our sight, what does that renewed sight make possible in actual life?

## Title coherence recommendations

Current titles are workable, but there is a slight inconsistency in style.

Right now some titles are:
- experiential (`On Fire`)
- diagnostic (`Nearsighted`)
- conceptual (`Human Lenses`, `God's Lenses`)
- theological (`Salvation`, `Sanctification`)
- practical (`Availability`)

That is not bad, but it can feel uneven.

Two options:

### Option A — keep current titles
This is fine if subtitles do the clarifying work.

### Option B — make subtitles more consistent
For example:
- On Fire — Awakening and Spiritual Disruption
- Nearsighted — The Problem of Distorted Sight
- Human Lenses — Why Blur Feels Normal
- God’s Lenses — The True Perspective
- Salvation — The Doorway, Not the Finish Line
- Sanctification — Vision Renewal
- Availability — Living With Farsight

Recommendation:
Keep the main titles, but tighten subtitles for consistency.
